How Much Does a Clipping Campaign Cost? Cost per 1,000 Views Explained

Learn how rates per 1,000 views work, what changes a campaign’s total cost, and how ClipRise pricing is structured.

By Abir Ahmed · Published October 29, 2026 · Last updated October 29, 2026

How clipping campaigns are priced

Many clipping campaigns use a rate per 1,000 views, often called CPM. The campaign sets the budget, rate, eligible platforms, and what counts as an approved view. Other campaigns may pay a fixed amount per post or use another model, so check the written terms.

As a simple rate calculation, $0.75 per 1,000 approved views would equal $750 for one million approved views before considering a campaign’s budget, minimums, payout caps, or other terms. ClipRise’s current minimum campaign budget is $1,000.

What affects the cost per 1,000 views?

  • Campaign requirements. Editing style, platforms, and additional deliverables can change the work involved.
  • Who manages the campaign. A brand-run marketplace means the brand handles more of the setup and review. A managed community handles coordination and view review.
  • View-review rules. Confirm how views are checked and what happens to invalid or botted views.
  • Budget and payout limits. Campaigns may set minimum budgets, per-post limits, or an overall cap.

What published guides report

Third-party guides give different examples for different campaign types, and they are not official rate cards. An OpusClip guide published in August 2026 reported $1–$3 per 1,000 views for standard clipping campaigns and a $0.50–$1.50 snapshot for typical live campaigns in mid-2026. A Postiz guide published in April 2026 described $2–$3 per 1,000 as a “reasonable” campaign range. These reports vary by campaign and may become outdated.

Why checking views matters

A low rate is useful only when the view-review rules are clear. ClipRise reviews campaign views, approves real views, and rejects botted or invalid views. Rejected views do not count toward the campaign.

Questions to ask a clipping provider

  • Is pricing based on approved views, a fixed post rate, or a campaign budget?
  • How are views reviewed?
  • What happens to botted or invalid views?
  • Who sets the brief and checks submissions?
  • What is the minimum budget, and are there payout caps?

Plan a budget around approved views

A rate only describes the cost of each block of 1,000 approved views. To estimate the rate-based portion of a campaign, use: approved views ÷ 1,000 × rate. At $0.75 per 1,000, 100,000 approved views would equal $75 at that rate; one million would equal $750. This calculation illustrates the rate. It is not a prediction of reach, an earnings guarantee, or confirmation that a campaign can spend beyond its budget.

Set the budget and the rate together. The minimum ClipRise campaign is $1,000, and launching is $0 for now. The rate starts from $0.75 per 1,000 approved views. The campaign terms shown before launch govern how much is available, which views are eligible, and what is paid. A calculation should never replace those terms.

Questions to settle before a campaign starts

Write down the parts that change the financial result. Ask what counts as an approved view, when views are reviewed, whether invalid or botted activity is excluded, and how the campaign budget applies. Confirm the allowed platforms and any posting requirements at the same time. If a provider uses a different model, such as a flat fee per post, compare the complete offer rather than translating it into a CPM without enough information.

  • Rate: the amount per 1,000 approved views or the alternative payout model.
  • Budget: the total amount available and the campaign minimum.
  • Review: how valid and invalid views are treated.
  • Requirements: platforms, creative brief, hashtags, mentions, and other instructions.
  • Timing: the campaign dates and any review milestones stated in its terms.

Why a lower headline rate is not the whole comparison

Offers should be compared on the same basis. One price may be per approved view while another may be per post or include other work. Fees, review rules, campaign limits, and brand responsibilities can also differ. A simple side-by-side price graphic can be useful only when it links to current, comparable published rates. If a figure cannot be traced to a provider’s current terms, leave it out and say that rates vary.
